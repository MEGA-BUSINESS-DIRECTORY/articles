A London House Move Becomes Easier With Careful Planning, Practical Packing And Storage Solutions For Awkward Gaps Between Properties

By Renée on 08 AUGUST 2026

A house move can turn an otherwise ordinary week into a strange mixture of excitement, cardboard boxes and constant mental calculations. One minute you're thinking about where the sofa will go in the new place, and the next you're wondering whether the electricity has been arranged, whether the removal van will actually fit outside, or where on earth you're supposed to put everything if the old tenancy ends before the new home is ready. For Londoners especially, moving house can involve tight streets, busy schedules, limited parking and properties where every square metre matters, which makes preparation particularly important. HOLD Self Storage London can be a valuable part of that preparation for anyone who needs somewhere secure to keep belongings during the move. A successful move isn't simply about getting boxes from one address to another. It is about managing dozens of small tasks in the right order while leaving enough flexibility for the unexpected. With some forward planning, a sensible packing strategy and a backup plan for those awkward gaps between properties, the whole process can feel considerably less chaotic.

Packing becomes much easier once it stops being treated as one enormous job. Trying to empty an entire home in a weekend is likely to create confusion, particularly if belongings from different rooms become mixed together. Starting early allows people to pack the things they won't need immediately, such as seasonal clothing, decorative items, books and occasional kitchen equipment, while leaving everyday essentials accessible until closer to moving day. Each box should be labelled clearly with both its destination room and a brief description of what is inside. Colour coding can help too, especially when several rooms are involved. Heavy belongings should go into smaller boxes so they remain manageable, while lighter items can fill larger ones. Fragile possessions deserve extra protection, and valuable documents should ideally travel separately rather than disappearing into a random box. A little organisation at this stage can save an enormous amount of time later, because the first evening in a new home is much easier when people can immediately find bedding, toiletries, chargers, basic kitchen equipment and a change of clothes.

Create a First-Night Survival Box

The first night in a new home can be surprisingly chaotic, especially if everything has been packed into identical-looking boxes. A dedicated first-night box or bag can make a huge difference. It should contain the things people are most likely to need immediately, such as toiletries, medication, chargers, towels, basic clothes, toilet paper, snacks, bottled water and essential kitchen items. Bedding should also be kept somewhere easy to reach rather than buried underneath boxes. Families may want to add children's favourite toys or comfort items, while anyone working early the next morning should make sure coffee, breakfast supplies and work essentials are available. Keeping these items separate means nobody has to unpack half the property simply to find a toothbrush or phone charger.

Photograph the Property Before Leaving

Moving day is usually too busy for anyone to remember every detail of a property, so taking photographs before leaving can be surprisingly useful. Pictures of meter readings, the condition of rooms, appliances and any existing marks can create a useful record, particularly for renters dealing with a deposit. It is also worth photographing the new property before furniture and boxes arrive, especially if there are areas that may need attention later. These photographs don't need to become a huge project; a quick, organised record on a phone can be enough. Keeping them backed up somewhere safe also means they won't disappear if the phone is lost or damaged during the move. It is one of those small preparations that takes very little time but can prevent confusion later.

Keep Important Documents Close

Moving involves a surprising amount of paperwork, from tenancy agreements and property documents to identification, insurance information, removal arrangements and utility confirmations. Putting all of these documents into a box labelled “important” isn't enough if that box ends up buried underneath furniture. Important paperwork should travel separately and remain accessible throughout the move. Digital copies can also be useful, provided sensitive information is stored securely. This is especially important if the move involves temporary accommodation or storage, because people may need documents unexpectedly while their main belongings are packed away. A dedicated folder or secure document bag can make it much easier to find what is needed without opening dozens of boxes.

Plan for Children and Pets

A house move can be exciting for adults but confusing for children and stressful for pets. Young children may struggle with the disruption caused by boxes, unfamiliar rooms and constant activity, while pets can become unsettled by strangers entering the home and furniture disappearing. Having a simple plan can make the day calmer. Children's essentials should remain accessible, and pets may benefit from staying somewhere quiet and familiar during the busiest part of the move. Once at the new property, setting up a familiar space with their usual belongings can help them settle. Keeping some household possessions in secure storage can also reduce the amount of furniture and boxes being moved around at once, creating a less chaotic environment during the transition.

Have a Backup Plan for Delays

Even the most carefully organised move can encounter delays. A key may not be available on time, a removal van could be delayed, repairs may take longer than expected or the new property may not be ready for occupation. Having a backup plan doesn't mean expecting everything to go wrong; it simply means avoiding a situation where one delay causes an entire chain of problems. The timing of the move deserves just as much attention as the packing itself. London property transactions and rental arrangements don't always line up neatly, and even a carefully planned move can be affected by delays. A completion date may shift, a landlord may need additional time, or a new property might not be ready when the current accommodation needs to be vacated. This is one of the situations where having a storage plan can make a major difference. Removal arrangements should be organised well before moving day, particularly in London where access can sometimes be more complicated than expected. Narrow streets, parking restrictions, controlled zones, stairs, lifts and limited loading areas can all affect how easily belongings can be moved. It is worth checking these details with the removal company rather than assuming everything will work itself out. Large furniture should be measured before the move, especially if the new property has narrow doorways or awkward staircases. Any items that need dismantling should ideally be prepared in advance, with screws and fittings placed in clearly labelled bags. Keeping an inventory of larger belongings can also make it easier to check that everything has arrived. For people using storage temporarily, the same level of planning helps. Furniture and boxes should be packed in a way that makes sense for their expected length of stay, with belongings that may be needed soon placed somewhere accessible rather than buried at the back.

Utilities are another part of moving that can easily get pushed aside because they seem less urgent than packing boxes. Electricity, gas, water, broadband and other household services all need attention, and some providers require notice before a service can be transferred or installed. Taking meter readings at the old property and again at the new one can help keep records clear, while photographs can provide useful evidence of the readings on the relevant date. Broadband deserves particular attention because installation appointments may not always coincide with the day someone moves in. Anyone working from home should plan for this rather than assuming internet access will be ready immediately. It can also help to keep important account details and confirmation emails somewhere accessible rather than inside a sealed box. These small administrative tasks may not be exciting, but dealing with them before moving day prevents unnecessary headaches when attention is already focused on unpacking and settling in.

Cleaning deserves its own place on the moving checklist because both the old and new homes need attention. If possible, cleaning the new property before boxes and furniture arrive is much easier than trying to clean around everything once the move is underway. Floors, cupboards, bathrooms and kitchen surfaces are particularly worth tackling while rooms are empty. The old property may also need to be cleaned before keys are returned, especially if the tenancy agreement requires it. Rather than leaving everything until the final few hours, spreading cleaning tasks across several days can make the process much more manageable. Some people may choose to hire professional cleaners, while others prefer to handle the work themselves. Either way, having cleaning supplies kept separately from the packed household belongings means they remain available right until the end. A small “cleaning kit” can include basic products, cloths, bin bags, paper towels and anything else needed for those final jobs.

Temporary accommodation can make a move particularly challenging because it often means living between two worlds. A hotel, serviced apartment, friend's home or short-term rental may provide somewhere to sleep, but it rarely has enough space for an entire household's possessions. Trying to take everything along can quickly become impractical, especially when furniture, children's belongings, work equipment and multiple boxes are involved. This is where temporary storage becomes more than just a convenience. It allows people to take only what they genuinely need into their temporary accommodation while keeping the rest of their possessions safely stored until the permanent home is ready. Clothing, toiletries, important documents, work equipment and basic cooking items can travel with the household, while larger furniture, decorative belongings and boxes that aren't immediately required can stay in storage. This creates a much more manageable temporary environment and avoids the strange experience of trying to live normally while surrounded by possessions that have no proper place to go.

A person may have already handed back their keys but be unable to access the new property for several days or even weeks. The first few days after moving can be almost as demanding as the days before it. Once the boxes arrive, there is a temptation to unpack everything immediately, but that can quickly make a new home feel chaotic. A better approach is to prioritise the rooms and belongings needed first. Bedding, toiletries, essential kitchen equipment, basic clothing and chargers should be easy to locate, while decorative items and less important possessions can wait. Setting up the bedroom and bathroom early can make the first night much more comfortable, even if half the house remains in boxes. It can also help to keep one clearly marked essentials box for each household member. For families, having children's favourite belongings easily accessible can make the transition easier, while people working from home may want to prioritise their workspace. Anything that doesn't need immediate attention can remain packed until there is enough time to organise it properly rather than being unpacked simply for the sake of emptying boxes.

A good moving plan also allows for the possibility that the original plan will change. A removal company might arrive late, a key handover might take longer than expected, furniture might not fit exactly where planned, or a new property might need unexpected repairs before everything can be moved in. Building some flexibility into the schedule makes these situations much easier to handle. It can mean arranging storage before it becomes urgently necessary, keeping a few days of essential clothing separate, maintaining access to important documents and having a realistic budget for unexpected costs.
